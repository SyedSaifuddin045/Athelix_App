# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

All fitness enthusiasts, regardless of experience level. Beginners learning proper form and progression, intermediate lifters following structured programs, and advanced athletes tracking periodization. The unifying context: someone who cares about how they train and wants their app to keep up — not hold their hand, not talk down, not overwhelm with data they didn't ask for.

## Product Purpose

Athelix exists so that lifters stop guessing. Random workouts produce random results; structured progression produces real gains. The app replaces "what should I do today?" with a system that tells you exactly what to do, tracks whether it's working, and shows where you're falling behind — all without requiring a personal trainer or spreadsheets.

Success looks like: a user opens the app, knows exactly what session to run, executes it, and over weeks sees their numbers climb while their muscle balance evens out. They never wonder "is this working?" because the answer is always visible.

## Positioning

Athelix is the only workout app that shows you exactly what you're building — mesocycle-by-mesocycle, muscle-by-muscle — so every session has purpose and every week has proof.

## Brand Personality

Clean, focused, serious. Athelix is the app for people who treat training like a discipline, not a hobby. The voice is direct and knowledgeable — no cheerleading, no filler, no "great job!" after every set. Confidence through competence. Think: a well-run gym, not a fitness influencer's Instagram.

## Anti-references

Generic fitness app aesthetic: MyFitnessPal's cluttered data tables, Strava's social-feed energy, Nike Training Club's lifestyle photography and motivational quotes. Athelix is not a social platform or a content library — it's a tool.

Clinical/medical interface: rehab app vibes, clinical data dashboards, white-coat aesthetics. Athelix is for healthy people training hard, not patients recovering. The data should feel empowering, not diagnostic.

## Design Principles

1. **Purpose over decoration.** Every screen answers a training question. If a UI element doesn't help the user train better, it shouldn't exist.
2. **Confidence through clarity.** The user should feel in control, not guided. Show the information clearly and trust them to act on it.
3. **Earn the dark.** Dark mode isn't a vibe — it's practical for gym environments (reduced glare, focus on content). The palette earns its darkness through contrast and hierarchy, not just being black.
4. **Data that moves.** Progress data should feel alive — animate transitions, show change over time, make the numbers feel like they're going somewhere.
5. **One task at a time.** Never ask the user to do two things on the same screen. Focused interfaces beat feature-packed dashboards.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard usability — make it work, don't worry about formal WCAG level. Ensure sufficient contrast for gym lighting (bright overhead fluorescents), readable text sizes, and touch targets large enough for sweaty fingers. Color choices should remain distinguishable for common color vision differences, even without strict WCAG compliance.
